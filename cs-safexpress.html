Inbound Transshipment — Unloading Redesign for Safexpress

Safexpress's inbound transshipment process was clogged at the unloading stage — manual workflows, weak visibility for team leads, high error rates in scanning, delaying downstream dispatch. I led the design effort across hub visits, stakeholder workshops, and agile sprints to deliver a fully functional unloading management system.

Challenge

Modernizing operations with minimal disruption to familiar workflows

Unloading operations were challenged by inconsistent unloading times, limited real-time visibility, and reliance on manual coordination. Scanning mismatches and delayed system updates prevented supervisors from accurately tracking progress, resulting in reactive decision-making and frequent team idle periods.

Communication on the floor was largely manual — verbal updates and phone calls — leading to confusion, misalignment across teams, and avoidable downtime. Reliability and usability issues with the handheld application disrupted workflows and reduced confidence in system data, further impacting productivity and data accuracy.

The lack of clear performance metrics and real-time dashboards limited effective performance monitoring and accountability across shifts. Overall, insufficient automation and digital transparency created operational inefficiencies, reduced labour utilisation, and hindered consistent execution of unloading activities.

Approach

Ride-along research leads to empathy and uncovering opportunities

To observe and document the workflows at various functional areas, the team and I split up to different areas of the warehouse to shadow employees. Over the course of a few days of shadowing, we gathered dozens of photos, pages of notes, and videos. Most interesting were the various paper-based workarounds that had been created to fill gaps in the system.

During the stakeholder workshop and hub visit, the team provided a walkthrough focusing on how goods are received and unloaded at the hub. The session helped visualise the complete operational flow — from arrival and dock allocation to unloading, sorting, and movement to outbound areas.

User Research

User Interview of Team Members

Team Member — Unloader (Primary User): Objective: To understand the actual pain points and behavioural patterns of team members who perform physical unloading and scanning at the transshipment hub. Key insights: Assignments are unclear until verbally told. Multiple clicks needed for each scan slows workflow. Wants a simple, fast interface with minimal steps.

Experienced Team Member (4+ yrs): Key insights: More paper notes when app fails to sync. Works faster with visual cues and fewer forms. Wants sound/vibration feedback for successful scans.

New Team Member (joined <6 months): Key insights: Finds app too technical and confusing. Icons unclear and actions take too long. Needs step-by-step guided flow for beginners.

Shift Lead — Field Supervisor: Key insights: Relies on manual headcount and verbal updates. No visibility of who is idle or busy. Wants system-generated task rotation and alerts.

Research Synthesis

Outcome of interviews — 3 core opportunity areas

The combined interviews revealed three core opportunity areas that directly informed the redesign: 1 — Real-Time Transparency: A live dashboard for supervisors and a visible queue for the team. 2 — Simplified Workflow: Reduced navigation steps and offline scan caching. 3 — Empowered Users: Self-assigned next tasks and performance tracking for motivation.

Re-approach

Product visioning workshops pave the way for a clearer future

Over the course of a week, the project leadership and I led a series of visioning workshops to level-set on the scope of the project and deep-dive into each functional area. Armed with several big whiteboards, I facilitated discussions with the client's functional team and subject-matter experts to define Happy Path and Unhappy Paths for each functional area. We then did high-level sketches of key screens.

Since the week-long visioning workshops were not initially part of the project timeline, I also reworked the design team timeline to account for the change in scope as well as an extension of the project. Having all this information made visible helped align all stakeholders and gave the design team more solid requirements to work from.

Design

A versatile and robust system that follows UX best practices

The Unloading Dashboard was designed to make everything visible when team members start an unloading session. Clear visual hierarchy for quick scanning. Real-time status updates. Optimised for Zebra handheld Android devices.

Key Features — Category Search & Tabs: Team members see a clear list of trucks assigned to them for the shift with priority indicators. Quick Scanning: Streamlined waybill scanning with exception handling for damaged or mislabelled packages. Vehicle Progress: Final sign-off and status change once all items are unloaded and verified.

Outcome

A fully functional system and an eager client team with new skills

We successfully delivered a fully functional unloading management system that is currently being piloted in several warehouses and sets the foundation for additional features for the system to be scaled up. The client's functional team gained new skills in Agile methodology and user story writing, enabling them to continue iterating effectively with the development team.

Reflection

Addressing the elephant in the room saves more time in the future

When the team first decided to pause to conduct the product visioning workshops, I was worried about how much that would impact our timelines. The design team was already behind schedule on several features due to churn with the requirements. However, stopping to re-visit the scope and user journeys ended up being just what we needed to move faster.

The 1-week pause in designing led to much more clarity and greatly reduced the number of iterations needed for each design flow. Sometimes, the fastest way to move forward is to stop and take a look around first.
