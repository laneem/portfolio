Inbound Transshipment — Unloading Redesign for Safexpress

Safexpress's inbound transshipment process was clogged at the unloading stage — manual workflows, weak visibility for team leads, high error rates in scanning. I led the design effort to discover warehouse workflows, create intuitive mobile interfaces, and guide the team through an agile design process.

Challenge

Modernizing operations with minimal disruption

Unloading operations were challenged by inconsistent unloading times, limited real-time visibility, and reliance on manual coordination. Scanning mismatches and delayed system updates prevented supervisors from accurately tracking progress. Communication on the floor was largely manual — verbal updates and phone calls — leading to confusion and avoidable downtime.

Research Approach

Ride-along research leads to real empathy

The team and I split up across different areas of the warehouse to shadow employees. Over several days, we gathered dozens of photos, pages of notes, and videos. Most revealing were the paper-based workarounds employees had created to fill gaps in the system — proof that the existing digital tools weren't trusted.

Re-approach

Product visioning workshops pave the way

When user stories kept hitting roadblocks, I paused to run a week-long series of product visioning workshops. Armed with whiteboards, I facilitated discussions with the client's functional team and subject-matter experts to define Happy Path and Unhappy Paths for each functional area. This one pause saved far more time than it cost.

Design

A versatile, robust system that follows UX best practices

The Unloading Dashboard was designed to make finding trucks, scanning waybills, and viewing status easy with clear information hierarchy. Real-time status updates, optimized for Zebra handheld Android devices. The system is currently being piloted in several warehouses and sets the foundation for additional features to be scaled across the network.

Reflection

Sometimes the fastest way forward is to stop

When the team first decided to pause for the product visioning workshops, I was worried about timelines. But stopping to revisit the scope ended up being exactly what we needed. The one-week pause led to much more clarity and greatly reduced the number of iterations needed. Sometimes the fastest way to move forward is to stop and look around first.
