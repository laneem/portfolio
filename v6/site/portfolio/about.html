About

I help product teams turn complicated, high-stakes workflows into software people can actually operate.

The short version

For eight years I've designed enterprise software in domains where the cost of a confusing interface is real — a banking permission that breaks compliance, a clinical-trial form that delays a study, a logistics dock that backs up an entire network. My work sits at the front of the product, not the end of it: I'm in the room for the discovery and the trade-offs, then I own the system that makes the answer scale. I've led design for platforms at SBI, Roche, Telefónica, and Safexpress, and built the design systems and research practice that let small teams ship without thrashing.

I'm most useful where the problem is genuinely hard and the users aren't like me — warehouse staff, trial coordinators, bank administrators — and the job is to design for how they actually work, not how a clean Figma file wishes they did.

How I work

3 principles
01 — Research

Design for the floor, not the demo.

Enterprise users are mid-task, under time pressure, and rarely sitting where you imagine. I get to the actual context — the dock, the support queue, the admin console at 6pm — through contextual inquiry and usability testing, and I let what people do on the worst day, not the happy path, set the requirements.

02 — Systems

Make one good decision, then make it scale.

In complex products the win isn't a clever screen — it's a modular system that holds up across roles, states, and brands. I design the common pattern once, define how it adapts to context, and document it so engineering can extend it without me. Consistency is the feature.

03 — Judgement

Hold the tension between user and business out loud.

Most real decisions are trade-offs — the safe option versus the usable one, what ships now versus what's right. I make the tension explicit, show what I considered and ruled out, and bring stakeholders to a decision they understand rather than a mockup they approve. That's the part that actually moves a roadmap.
